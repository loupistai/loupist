# Peer analysis

Adapted from Anthropic financial-services, comps-analysis (Apache-2.0).
See [upstream provenance](../../NOTICE.md).

## Select and align

Choose peers by business model and relevant exposure before looking for a
favorable valuation. Explain differences in product mix, size, geography,
cyclicality, growth, profitability, and leverage. Separate weak comparables
rather than quietly treating them as equivalent.

Align fiscal periods, LTM/NTM basis, currency, units, consolidation, and
reported/adjusted definitions. Distinguish forecast estimates and their dates
from actuals. Reconcile share counts, market capitalization, and enterprise
value at a consistent market date. Preserve meaningful missing observations.

## Choose metrics

Use sector-appropriate measures: software may need recurring revenue and
retention; industrials may need capital intensity and backlog; banks often need
ROE, capital quality, and book-value-based measures rather than EBITDA.
Choose metrics that answer this question; a fixed column count is unnecessary.

Pair enterprise value with operating measures such as revenue or EBITDA and
equity value with measures attributable to common holders. Reconcile leases,
minority interests, and adjusted earnings definitions. A negative or zero
multiple denominator is not a cheap positive multiple; mark it not meaningful
and explain an alternative when useful.

## Summarize and check

Preserve peer-level inputs and calculations. Use median and dispersion when
they describe the sample; disclose sample size, excluded observations, and
whether averages are weighted. Aggregate margins by aggregating numerators and
denominators when measuring a combined business, rather than averaging ratios.

Explain a premium or discount using the operating comparison and evidence.
Do not transfer the peer median mechanically. Margin ordering, a fixed multiple
range, and a zero fallback for division errors are not universal validity checks.
Recompute representative rows and the applied multiple-to-equity bridge.
