# EXTRACTION RULES

1. **Entity Name Validation**: `source_entity_name` and `target_entity_name` must use only the `name` values from the ENTITIES list provided above.
   - **CRITICAL**: Using names not in the list will cause the edge to be rejected
2. A value reported about one subject (a figure, a guidance range, a rating) is a REPORTS fact about that subject: use the subject as both `source_entity_name` and `target_entity_name`. Any other relation needs two different entities from the ENTITIES list.
3. When a sentence states a specific detail about an entity (a product, a place, a counterparty, an amount, a condition), do NOT drop it. Relate the entity to the entity in the ENTITIES list that the detail names; when the detail is a value, keep it as a fact about its subject.
4. Do not emit semantically redundant facts. A statement that adds a specific detail to another (an amount, a period, a counterparty, a condition) is a NEW fact, not a duplicate; statements that convey the same specifics are one fact.
5. The `fact` MUST preserve all specific details from the source text: proper nouns, product names, model numbers, quantities, units, currencies, periods, conditions and named locations. Paraphrase the sentence structure but NEVER generalize ("LFP cathode material" stays, not "battery material"; "three plants" stays, not "several plants").
   - Do not verbatim quote the original text, but every concrete noun, number, and descriptor in the source should survive into the `fact`.
6. Do **not** hallucinate or infer temporal bounds from unrelated events.

# DATETIME RULES

- `valid_at` is when the stated relationship began and `invalid_at` is when it ended. Set one only when the CURRENT MESSAGE states that time, or gives it relative to the present ("last month", "since March") so that it resolves against REFERENCE_TIME. Otherwise leave it `null`.
- Present tense alone sets no time. Neither does REFERENCE_TIME, a publication date, or the start or end of a reporting period.
- Keep the precision the text gives: a year (2025), a month (2025-04), a date (2025-04-30), or an instant in ISO 8601 with a "Z" suffix (2025-04-30T14:30:00Z). Never add a time of day, or a first day of a month or year, that the text does not state.
- A numeric date whose day and month could be swapped (05/06/2025) states neither: unless the text itself settles the order, give `valid_at` or `invalid_at` only the year, and keep the date as written in the `fact`.
- REFERENCE_TIME may be followed by `reference_basis`. `published` means it is when the Source was published. `received` means it is only when the Source was received, which may be long after it was written: resolve a relative expression against a date the text gives for itself, and leave the time `null` when it gives none.
