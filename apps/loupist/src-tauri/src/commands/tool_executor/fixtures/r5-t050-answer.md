At Solsup Foods’ April 16, 2026 KRX close of [₩21,950](#evidence-ev_quote), a reverse DCF implies **[2.1%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) annual free-cash-flow growth over five years** in the central case. The range is [−4.5%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) to [7.4%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) across the assumptions below. Even the highest implied rate is below the company’s [10.0%](#evidence-ev_cagr) annual growth in the same cash-flow measure from FY2020 to FY2025.

| Assumed WACC | 1% terminal growth | 2% terminal growth | 3% terminal growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8% | [1.3%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) | [−1.4%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) | [−4.5%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) |

| 10% | [7.4%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) | [5.3%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) | [2.9%](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf) |

The model starts with FY2025 consolidated operating cash flow less property, plant and equipment purchases, [₩33.7bn](#evidence-ev_fcf), as a proxy for unlevered free cash flow. It adds FY2025 year-end net borrowings of [₩53.8bn](#evidence-ev_net_debt) to the closing market cap of [₩439bn](#evidence-ev_quote) to get an implied enterprise value of [₩492.8bn](#evidence-ev_reverse_dcf).

The close did not require a repeat of historical cash-flow growth under any of these scenarios. That conclusion depends on FY2025 cash flow being a representative starting point; the implied rates are not forecasts.
